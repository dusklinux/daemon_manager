# Logging and remaining candidates — 2026-10-13

This follows the [main audit](2026-10-13.md), including the reproduced delayed searchd-Off Photos CPU loop. Known CPU/dependency failures remain Critical. Unverified candidates are not equivalent to verified safe disabling, and ordinary feature loss is distinct from a pathological CPU loop.

## Measured logging cost and existing opt-outs

On the installed iOS 16.7.16 phone, three roughly 10-second samples measured logd at 0.103%, 0.098%, 0.121% of one core, with 6.92–6.98 MiB physical footprint. logd_helper used 0% in those intervals and 1.63 MiB. These are per-process readings, not net RAM/battery savings. The main logd job runs in system, not user/501.

analyticsd, aggregated, diagnosticd, diagnosticextensionsd, iosdiagnosticsd, osanalyticshelper, powerlogHelperd, syslogd and the previously disabled diagnostic collectors are already Off in the inspected profile. Optional analytics and legacy syslogd are distinct from the still-enabled unified logger. The logd plist exposes /var/run/syslog itself.

Apple documents that debug/info messages ordinarily remain in memory, while higher-severity messages are compressed/persisted within storage limits; [logging behavior](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/os/generating-log-messages-from-your-code). Logging has a cost, but silencing it does not fix the workload that produces errors. The earlier Photos loop was resolved by restoring searchd, not by hiding its messages. No debug profile, global logging setting or core logd Off override was applied in this follow-up.

## Isolated logd_helper check

The starting override database was preserved separately as disabled.before-logging.plist under the phone's Documents/daemon-audit-2026-10-13 directory. Only logd_helper was newly disabled, then userspace restarted. Its job was unregistered; logd and searchd stayed enabled and Siri remained Off. The user performed Photos/Camera, typing and WhatsApp checks, then turned off the screen. No passcode was introduced or changed.

Twelve 30-second resource intervals covered startup/use and subsequent screen-off activity, followed by two additional 30-second intervals. More than five minutes of screen-off observation showed no sustained one-core client retry loop; pressure remained Normal. In the nine later intervals of the first run, logd used 0.052–0.203% of one core, assetsd 0–1.268%, SpringBoard 0.093–1.276%, and backboardd 0.435–2.541%. The final two intervals had backboardd at 0.473%, 0.468%; the main logger and Photos did not enter a sustained high-CPU loop. This is a scoped pass, not long-term maintenance validation.

Native logd explicitly references logd_helper. The helper binary contains ownership-change and deletion operations and a helper-image limit; these observations indicate a maintenance role, without establishing the complete private protocol. Rotation, metadata ownership and cleanup over days or low storage were not exercised. Recent persisted trace files continued to change during Off, but that does not validate every retention/maintenance path. A private recent-trace decode covered part of the experiment; it did not contain logger/helper-specific records and had decoder limitations. Absence of those records does not establish clean logs.

Only logd_helper was then enabled and bootstrapped again. Its builtin target stays On. No measured pathological failure justifies a new Critical claim for it; the small short-term footprint disappearance is insufficient to recommend routine Off. Main logd remains On and was not disabled in this test. No kernel/full reboot was performed.

## Critical classification correction

The actual DNS labels are com.apple.mDNSResponder.reloaded and com.apple.mDNSResponderHelper.reloaded. Their installed plists point to the same mDNSResponder/helper executables covered by the older Critical names. Descriptions already mapped these labels, but SafetyManager did not; consequently their rows lacked the expected Critical warning. v1.0.25 adds the two exact labels to the existing Critical policy, tests that they retain warning/On targets, and changes their config markers to uppercase NO. This is a label-policy correction, not a new disabling/CPU experiment. Their live overrides and On targets are unchanged.

Builtins remain 512 entries, 315 Off / 197 On; Critical enabled entries increase from 88 to 90 because the two installed labels now carry their existing legacy policy. The target inventory CSV updates classification only for those rows; its initial phone-state columns remain historical. No additional builtin target was changed to Off.
